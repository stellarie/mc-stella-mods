# Stella Nether Raid

An instanced nether raid. The design is in `docs/plans/2026-09-28/NETHER-RAID.md`.

## Slice 0: scaffold

- The mod requires the rest of the modpack: mobs, equips and enchants, spells, villages, the tower and food.
  Gradle builds each from its sibling directory as an included build, so build from this directory with
  the siblings checked out beside it.

## Slice 1: the raid dimension and the portal choice

- A second nether, `mod-stella-nether:raid`, generated by the nether's own noise and biomes.
- A player stepping into an overworld nether portal is held there and asked in chat: **[Normal nether]**
  or **[Raid nether]**. Clicking runs `/stellanether portal normal|raid`. The next crossing takes them
  there. A choice covers one crossing and lapses after 600 ticks (30 seconds). The player is asked again
  at most every 100 ticks while they stay in the portal.
- In survival, the prompt comes after vanilla's 4-second portal wait, and the crossing after another
  4 seconds.
- Mobs and other entities are never asked. They cross to the normal nether as in vanilla. A player riding
  a mount decides for the mount.
- The raid arrival is an obsidian hall at (0, 64, 1) with a lit portal out at its north end. Lava in the
  hall's shell is turned to blackstone.
- Inside the raid nether, every nether portal leads back beside the portal the player came in by. With no
  recorded entrance, it leads to their respawn point. Scrolls of Return work unchanged.

## Slice 2: host and mode

- The raid nether is one shared instance. Its state (run, open, host, mode, time run) is saved on the
  Overworld.
- While no raid is open, the portal prompt offers **[Host raid: normal]** and **[Host raid: hard]**; the
  first player through opens the raid, hosts it, and sets its mode. While a raid is open, it offers
  **[Join the raid (mode)]** instead, and a later player joins without changing host or mode.
- Every player inside holds one boon, drawn at random from speed, haste, strength, jump boost,
  regeneration, resistance, fire resistance, night vision, absorption, health boost, slow falling and
  luck. It lasts while they stay inside, and leaving takes it away. A potion of the same kind that is
  not endless is left alone.
- In hard mode, every hostile mob that loads in the raid holds two different boons, drawn from speed,
  strength, resistance, regeneration, absorption, health boost and fire resistance. Only effects the mob
  can hold are drawn, so undead are never handed regeneration. A mob is boosted once per run.
- Each run has its own site; see slice 5.

## Slice 3: the instance lifecycle

- The open raid counts its ticks, saved with its state.
- Once no player has stood in the raid for 100 ticks (5 seconds), it resets. Dying, a Scroll of Return, a
  portal and logging out all count as leaving. Loaded dropped items in the raid are discarded, and the
  next run opens 2048 blocks away, so everything left on the old run's land is lost for good.
- A player who logs back in inside a run that has since closed is sent back the way they came. A player
  who logs back in inside the live run stays.
- The old runs' land stays on disk. The raid dimension's region files grow by one explored area per run,
  and pruning them is a follow-up.

## Slice 4: the enrage

- At 30 minutes (36,000 ticks) of open raid, everyone inside is warned, and every hostile mob in the raid
  gains endless Speed II and Strength II and 64 more blocks of follow range. Zombified piglins count.
  A mob that loads later in the same run is enraged as it loads.
- Once a second, an enraged mob with no player to hunt takes the nearest survival or adventure player
  within 64 blocks. A neutral mob stays angry at them.
- The enrage lasts until the raid resets.

## Slice 5: a fortress and a bastion in reach

- Each run's origin lies 8,192 blocks from the last (800 runs per row, inside the nether's world border).
- The run opens midway between a fortress and a bastion of the raid nether's own generation that lie no
  more than 480 blocks apart, so both structure starts are within 256 blocks of the arrival. Nothing is
  forced into the land. The search reads the actual structure start in each placement cell within three
  cells of the origin (vanilla's locate is not used: it answers with the first ring holding a match, not
  the nearest), and takes the pair nearest the origin.
- Measured: 60 of 60 runs over five suite runs had both in reach (by a scan of every chunk within 256
  blocks), with no fallback. The same check at the bare origin, before this slice, gave 2 of 6.
- The search runs on the server thread the first time a run is needed, costing up to about 2 seconds
  (worst measured 2,044 ms) once per run.
- A server with structures switched off has no fortress or bastion to find; the run then opens at its
  origin and logs a warning.

## Slice 6a: the raid's threat level

- The mobs mod gives a dimension it does not know no threat floor, so raid mobs rolled threat 0 to 4,
  weaker than the normal nether's 20. Every mob that loads in the raid now has its threat lifted to start
  from the nether's floor (`ThreatState.NETHER_MINIMUM`), keeping its own roll above that, and is scaled
  again by the mobs mod's `SpawnScaling`. Measured: eight raid zombified piglins at levels 20 to 28,
  40 to 48 health.
- Fixed: the reset discarded items while walking the level's entity map, which skipped 23 of 64 items
  and once crashed the server tick (`ArrayIndexOutOfBoundsException` in `RaidInstance.tick`). Both
  loops over the raid's entities now walk a snapshot.

## Slice 6b: the Blaze Sovereign's body

- Once per run, the first time a player comes within 96 blocks of the run's fortress while its start
  chunk is loaded, the Blaze Sovereign rises there. It stands on the nearest open floor within 32 blocks
  of the fortress start (solid below, seven blocks of air above, no lava). It never rises again in that
  run, slain or not; a new run raises a new one.
- Health: 1000 plus 50 per threat level, where its threat is the raid roll lifted to the nether's floor
  plus the mobs mod's boss bonus of 10, so 2,500 to 3,000 in practice. The health attribute holds at most
  1,024; the rest is a divisor on every hit it takes, so a 100-point hit costs 100 of its whole health.
  It opts out of the mobs mod's spawn rolls, so its kit and scaling are its own.
- It stands three times a blaze's size (5.4 blocks) and keeps within two blocks of the ground below it,
  drawn down half a block a tick if higher.
- Every hit it lands, by fireball or touch, sets its target alight for 5 seconds.
- A blue, notched boss bar shows to every raid player within 96 blocks.
- Not yet: the blue tint needs client code. Its specials show blue soul-fire particles, which need none.

## Slice 7: the Sovereign's specials

While the Sovereign has a living target within 32 blocks, it casts a special 3 seconds after first
facing one, then another 8 seconds after each ends, in this turn (slices 12f and 14 changed some; see below):

1. **Flame Spiral**: for 5 seconds, a ring of eight fireballs every quarter second, turning as it goes:
   160 fireballs covering every direction, each flying 32 blocks at most.
2. **Brood**: five ordinary blazes rise in a ring around it and take its target.
3. **Dive**: it rises 8 blocks over 1 second, hangs half a second, then dives onto where its target stood,
   breaking every block it moves into.
   Everything alive in the 12 by 12 area takes 20 damage and burns for 8 seconds, and about a third of
   the ground in the area catches fire. Blazes are spared.
4. **Vortex** (replaced the Meteor Storm in slice 14; see there).
5. **Charged Wave**: a ring spreads from it at a block a tick out to 16 blocks, striking each creature it
   passes once: 4 damage, a push of 2.5 away, and a stun.

A stun is 3 seconds of Slowness VII, Mining Fatigue III and Weakness II. Every hit the Sovereign lands
also burns, as before.

## Slice 8: the spoils and the End

- A slain Sovereign always leaves a **Blaze Core**, and with 5% chance a **Flame Mantle** as well.
  Measured: 4.91% over 40,000 seeded rolls.
- The **Flame Mantle** is a chestplate with netherite's protection (8 armor, 3 toughness), repaired with
  Blaze Cores, and takes armor enchantments. Worn, it heals its wearer 1 extra health every 2 seconds,
  sets whoever strikes its wearer alight for 4 seconds, and takes 80% of fire damage away. It uses
  netherite's look and a vanilla icon until it has art of its own.
- The End seal of this slice (three cores in an End portal) is replaced by slice 10's sealed chest.

## Slice 9: more from the raid's loot

- Every loot table rolled in the open raid nether, from mobs, blocks and chests alike, gives x1.15 in
  normal and x1.5 in hard; the fraction past a whole count is rolled. Measured on nether quartz ore over
  2,000 breaks: 1.135 in normal, 1.5015 in hard, 1.0 outside the raid.
- The raid never ends while a player is inside, so a party can farm it or go for the Sovereign.

## Slice 10: the sealed chest

- Every stronghold's portal room holds one **Sealed Chest**: black, unbreakable, immune to blasts,
  withers and the dragon, and with no item. Measured: a placed stronghold holds exactly one, standing on
  solid floor with all 12 frames within 16 blocks.
- The eyes go in first, as in vanilla, but the last eye leaves the portal shut while no chest within 16
  blocks holds a Blaze Core. Setting **one** Blaze Core in the chest (use it on the chest) opens the
  portal at once. The core is spent, the chest glows and takes no more, and the portal stays open.
- A stronghold generated before this mod has no chest; completing its frame raises one on the frame.

## Slice 11: the Blaze Core's enchantments

A weapon or armor piece on the left of an anvil and a Blaze Core on the right sets one of these, for the
core and 10 levels. Neither comes from the enchanting table or from loot.

- **Overclock** (weapons): each hit on the same enemy strikes 10% harder than the first, per earlier hit
  in the row, up to +100% at the eleventh. A hit on any other enemy starts over. Its buff shows the row.
  Measured: 2.0, 2.2, 2.4, 2.6 on one enemy, then 2.0 on another.
- **Magma Armor** (armor, any piece): every hit taken, from any source, is lessened by what the armor has
  built, and then builds 1% more, up to 30%, separate from armor points. Five seconds untouched, it cools
  2% a second. Its buff shows the percent. Measured: 10% after ten hits, 30% at the cap, and 10% cooling
  to 6.1% over two seconds past the delay.

## Slice 12a: the Sovereign burns blue

- The mod now has a client side (`src/client`), so it goes on clients as well as the server.
- The Sovereign's mark syncs to every client, and the client draws its model in soul-fire blue.
- Every fireball the Sovereign looses, from its own attack or a special, flies as a blue **Soul Fire
  Charge** instead of a fire charge. Measured: 160 of 160 spiral fireballs and a loose one.
- The tint is the client's work and cannot be seen by a server test: its test
  (`theSovereignIsDrawnBlueRequiresManualTest`) is not required and fails every run until checked by hand.
  A client could not be started here (no OpenGL), so the client mixins are checked only against the
  client jar's method signatures.

## Slices 12b to 12d: the raid's mobs

- **A plain brood**: the Sovereign's Brood are ordinary blazes. They take none of the mobs mod's spawn
  rolls, so none is elite or boss. Measured: 0 of 50 ranked even with every blaze's elite and boss chance
  pinned to 1.0 (50 of 50 without the change).
- **Scrolls home**: any mob slain in the raid leaves the spells mod's Scroll of Return 1% of the time.
  Measured: 0.01002 over 100,000 rolls. The Sovereign leaves one per player in the raid, plus two:
  4 with two players.
- **Strength by mode**: every raid enemy but the Sovereign has x1.25 health and damage in normal and
  x1.75 in hard, and starts at full health. The Sovereign keeps its own formula.

## Slice 12e: the Sovereign's burst

- The Sovereign's normal attack is a burst of five blue fireballs, 4 ticks apart, starting every 5 seconds.
  It replaces vanilla's blaze attack and the mobs mod's steady beat, and holds while a special runs.
  Measured: shots at ticks 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, then 120 to 136, in each of five runs.

## Slice 12f: the soul nova

- A sixth special, last in the rotation. Eighteen slow blue flames (a ring of twelve, and six angled
  upward) drift out at 3 blocks a second.
- Each one bursts on its own, at whichever comes first: a wall, 16 blocks out, or 6 seconds. It hurts (6)
  and throws back whatever stands within 3 blocks, and sets three soul flames on the floor around it. It
  breaks no block.
- The soul flame is the mod's own block, drawn with vanilla's soul-fire models. It holds on any solid
  floor, burns like soul fire, and goes out after 10 seconds.
- Measured: 18 flames over 8 directions; in open air a burst at tick 106 after drifting 16.05 blocks; a
  flame meeting a wall 6 blocks out burst at tick 36, 5.40 blocks out; a stand-in pushed (1.5, 0.4, 0)
  and hurt; 46 to 53 soul flames set, and no floor block broken.

## Slice 12h: the opening (replaced)

- The once-only opening charge was replaced in slice 14 by the charge at a player keeping away.

## Slice 12i: the portal window

- A client with the mod gets the portal's choice as a window of three stacked options: Normal nether, Host
  raid: normal, Host raid: hard. While a raid runs, the one matching its mode reads Join the raid, and the
  other is greyed out. A client without the mod keeps the chat prompt.
- The window opens the moment a player steps into an overworld nether portal, not after the portal's wait.
  It is sent once and stays up while they stand in the portal, until they click an option or cancel.
- A click sends the player through the portal at once. Cancelling (Escape) sets the player down 2 blocks
  from the portal's plane on the side they came from (the other side if that is blocked), with a small
  shove and a knock sound. Stepping back in opens the window again.
- Bug fixed: the window used to flash and vanish. The client closes any screen that does not allow itself
  in a portal, and the window now does.
- Measured: the first step into the portal opened the window, and five more steps plus the portal's own
  wait sent no second one. A cancel set the player 2.000 blocks from the plane and closed the window.
- Measured: the hard option sent a player into a new hard raid on the same tick. A normal option during a
  hard raid sent no one and left no choice behind. Both messages survive the wire.
- The window itself is the client's: its tests (`thePortalWindowOpensAndClicksRequiresManualTest` and
  `theWindowStaysOpenWhileStandingInThePortalRequiresManualTest`) are not required and fail every run
  until checked by hand.

## Slice 12g: the raid's own spawns

- Every 2 seconds, each player in the raid is tried for a spawn 16 to 40 blocks away:
  - In either mode, a blaze rises from the nearest lava surface with open air above it.
  - In hard mode, a wither skeleton appears on any solid, dry floor with three blocks of room, not only in
    a fortress.
- Each kind stops at 8 within 64 blocks of the player. They are ordinary spawns, so the mobs mod's rolls
  apply to them.
- Measured on each run's own terrain around the arrival, 50 attempts each, over five gate runs:
  - Normal: 50 blazes and 0 wither skeletons every time.
  - Hard: 29 to 50 blazes and 26 to 50 wither skeletons; the terrain decides.
  - In play, an attempt comes every 2 seconds per player, so near lava a new blaze appears about every 2
    seconds until 8 stand near.

## Slice 12j: raid casters

- In either mode, three raid mobs cast through the spells mod, on its own cooldowns:
  - A wither skeleton strikes with Ember Brand (burns) and sings Battle Hymn (Strength) on itself.
  - A piglin brute rings itself in Flame Ring (burns what stands near) and turns to Stoneguard (Resistance).
  - A ghast casts Time Lock (Slowness) at its target and Mending Word (heals) on itself when hurt.
- The casting runs beside each mob's own attack, not in place of it.
- Measured: each caster cast both of its spells at a stand-in. The wither skeleton's and the brute's
  targets burned, the ghast's was slowed, and the buffs showed on the casters (Strength, Resistance).

## Slice 14: the Sovereign reworked

- **No clipping.** Whatever the Sovereign moves itself into, in a dive or a charge, it breaks as it goes,
  dropping nothing; only unbreakable blocks stand. Shut in and suffocating, it bursts out: an explosion
  (power 4) that breaks the blocks around it, and whatever that leaves around its body is broken too.
- **The charge** replaces the opening. Any player within 96 blocks but farther than 20 is charged at 8
  blocks a second, through walls, until the Sovereign is within 10 blocks and sees them. Then comes a
  volley of nine blue fireballs over 3 seconds. It charges again 10 seconds after each volley, as often as
  a player keeps away. Its attack and specials wait while a charge runs.
- **Reaches.** Spiral fireballs are spent at 32 blocks. Soul nova flames burst at a wall, 16 blocks or
  6 seconds (slice 12f above).
- **The vortex** replaces the Meteor Storm:
  - For 12 seconds, players inside a 32 by 32 square are pulled slowly toward the Sovereign. A mix of
    blue and yellow flames flies in from the square's edge, faster than the pull.
  - A flame ends only when it hits a player or reaches the centre. Blue flames burst like the soul nova;
    yellow ones burn (4 damage, 4 seconds alight).
  - Then the Sovereign takes a shield worth 200 of its whole health, as absorption. While it holds, the
    Sovereign can neither move nor attack, and 2 to 4 plain blazes appear in the square every 5 seconds,
    12 alive at most. Breaking the shield ends it.
- **A debug command**, for game masters: `/stellanether sovereign summon` raises a Blaze Sovereign where it
  is run, in the raid nether only.
- Measured:
  - Charge: 0.400 blocks a tick through a wall (18 blocks broken), ending 10.00 away and in sight. The
    volley was nine shots over ticks 1 to 54, all blue. A player who moved away again was charged again.
  - Dive: it broke the 9 ceiling blocks it rose into. Shut in stone, it burst out once, its body clear.
  - Spiral: fireballs down three tunnels were spent at 33.22 blocks (the tick they passed 32).
  - Vortex: 180 flames, all from the edge, a mix of blue and yellow, each ending at the centre or on a
    player. The pull pointed at the centre. A blue flame burst on the player it hit. The shield held 200.0,
    the Sovereign moved 0.000 and fired nothing, and waves of 2 to 4 came until a blow broke the shield.

## Slice 15: blazes keep their distance

- Every blaze but the Sovereign now keeps low and keeps its distance: it never flies up toward a target,
  it closes in when its target is farther than 10 blocks, and backs away when the target comes within 5.
  Its attack is unchanged.
- This is the mobs mod's creature, but the change lives in this mod (a mixin on the blaze) because this
  work is confined to its branch; it applies wherever this mod is installed.
- A blaze in the air (lifted by a blast, or settling slowly as blazes do) moves under its own power, at
  0.12 blocks a tick, along the way it means to go.
- Measured:
  - Set down 2 blocks from its target, a blaze backed out to 5.14 to 6.34 blocks in each of two rounds.
  - From 20 blocks, it closed to 10.41 to 10.50.
  - With a target 6 blocks above it, it rose 0.000 over 200 ticks; without the change, a blaze climbed
    7.8 to 10.1.
- The four-round version of the back-off test
  (`aBlazeBacksAwayEveryTimeOverFourRoundsRequiresManualTest`) is tagged, not required. Its fourth round
  came up short once in five runs, after five fixes. The blaze is shoved about by the blasts of its own
  fireballs at a target that close.

## Manual acceptance

- Walk into an overworld nether portal in survival with the mod on the client. The window opens with three
  options stacked, as soon as you step in, and it stays up. Clicking one sends you at once; Esc steps
  you 2 blocks back out of the portal. Without the mod on
  the client, the chat prompt appears instead, and clicking **[Raid nether]** takes you to the hall. The headless test player is never ticked, so the tests call the portal's
  destination directly and cannot walk through it.
- The prompt options are coloured, and hovering shows what each one means. The options change once a
  raid is open.
- A boon shows in the inventory's effect list as endless while inside, and is gone after leaving.
- Start the client with the mod: it reaches the title screen (the client mixins apply), and the
  Sovereign is drawn blue, its fireballs blue orbs.
- Walk to the fortress in a new run: the Sovereign rises there, its bar appears, and its fireballs burn.
- Stand inside the vortex: you feel the pull on your own client (the headless test sees only the pull it
  is given), the blue and yellow flames read clearly, and the shield's standstill and its blazes are plain.
- The Sovereign's burst reads as five quick blue shots, then a pause.
- Keep your distance from a Sovereign: it breaks through the fortress toward you, then fires its volley
  of nine. Back away again and it comes again.
- Watch ordinary blazes: they stay low, hang back at 5 to 10 blocks, and back off when you close in. Close
  in on one again and again: it backs off every time (the tagged four-round test).
- Summon a Sovereign with `/stellanether sovereign summon` in the raid nether: it rises where you stand.
- Stand near a lava lake in the raid: blazes rise out of it. In a hard raid, wither skeletons turn up in
  open caves away from the fortress.
- Fight a brute and a wither skeleton in the raid: the spells' particles and sounds read clearly.
- The soul nova's flames read as slow blue orbs, and their soul flames draw as soul fire (a cut-out
  texture, not a black square) and go out after 10 seconds.
- Fight the Sovereign through all six specials. Each reads clearly: the spiral's rings, the brood, the
  dive's rise and crash, the vortex, the wave's ring, and the nova. A pushed player flies back on their own
  client (the headless test sees only the velocity it is given).
- Slay the Sovereign: a Blaze Core drops. Wear a Flame Mantle and stand in fire; strike a mob and watch
  it burn back.
- Combine a sword and a Blaze Core at an anvil (Overclock), then a chestplate and a core (Magma Armor);
  the buffs show their icons and levels while fighting.
- In a new world's stronghold: the black chest stands in the portal room. Fill the eyes (the portal stays
  shut, with a message), then use a Blaze Core on the chest: the portal opens. The chest's look is built
  from blackstone textures until it has art of its own.
- From a new run's arrival, a fortress and a bastion are both in sight within a short walk.
- After the enrage, a crowd of zombified piglins actually chases a player back to the hall's portal.
  The test proves the targeting, not the pathing through nether terrain.
